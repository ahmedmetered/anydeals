# Method — writing the emails

Distilled from Harvey's email_frameworks.md (MIT) with the 2026 reply-rate findings. Rules here are not stylistic suggestions; they are the deliverability and trust floor.

## The only rule that matters

Write like a real person who noticed something and is reaching out. Read every draft out loud: if it sounds like marketing or AI, rewrite it.

## Compliance (overrides everything)

- Truthful subject lines. No fake "re:"/"fwd:", no implied prior relationship.
- Real identity only: sender name and company match persona.md exactly.
- Every campaign needs a working opt-out; any opt-out wording ("stop", "remove me", "not interested") is honored immediately and permanently.
- Plain text only. No attachments, no images/pixels, no formatting (no bold, italics, bullets, headers). At most one link per email, zero in email 1.
- EU/UK: B2B cold email needs a defensible legitimate-interest basis. The pitch must be genuinely relevant to the recipient's professional role and email 1 must make objecting easy. If relevance is a stretch, do not send.

## Email 1 — the observation (under 75 words, count them)

1. One specific, verifiable detail about THEM (from their site/listing). Not a compliment.
2. One line connecting it to a problem or opportunity.
3. One real question they'd want to answer. No product name, no pitch.

## Email 2 — the angle (day 4-6, under 75 words, new subject, new thread)

A completely different angle. Never "following up". One plain problem statement, one concrete cost/stat (only if true), one line on how it's solved (product MAY be named here), one direct question.

## Email 3 — the bump (day 9-11, under 40 words)

Two sentences: still relevant? Close the loop like a human. No pitch in the goodbye, no guilt ("you're probably losing X" is a guilt trip, not a breakup).

## Breakup (day 14)

Acknowledge, leave the door open, stop. 6 touches max per sequence, then never again.

## Subjects

Lowercase, 2-5 words, no recipient name, must read like an internal email. Banned: "quick question" (and equivalents), anything hype. New subject per email in a sequence.

## Reply triggers — every email is built around exactly ONE

1. Recognition: their problem named in their words. Strongest; requires real specificity.
2. Curiosity gap: you know something they'd want to know. Only if you deliver the payoff on reply. Never fake it.
3. Easy yes: replying costs less than deleting ("is this already handled?").
4. Peer proof: a named, public, TRUE peer did the relevant thing.

## Banned patterns (scan every draft)

Em dashes. "Picture this", "imagine if", "what if I told you", "here's what I keep seeing". Before/after lists. Feature dumps (3+ features). "Worth a quick call/look/chat?". "I hope this finds you well", "just following up", "I'd love to", "reaching out because", "just wanted to". Bullet points. Starting with "I" or "We" (start with them). Exclamation marks. Multiple questions. "No pressure/obligation". Parenthetical asides. Buzzwords: game-changer, seamless, leverage, unlock, elevate, robust, effortless, delve.
Spam words (never): free, guarantee(d), risk-free, act now, limited time, exclusive, discount, % off, save big, winner, congratulations, urgent, last chance, click here, sign up now, make money, increase sales, double your, 100%, $$$.
Sounding-human extras: no "Not X, but Y" contrasts (state Y), no one-line dramatic closers (end on the question), no staged run-ups ("Here's the thing:"), no forced triads, no -ing riders tacked on sentences, straight quotes only, vary sentence length.

## 2026 data to write by

Replies peak at 50-125 words; 200+ words reply at half the rate. Offer-CTAs ("want the teardown?") beat meeting-asks by ~28%; easy-yes CTAs beat big asks. Short bumps nearly double replies vs re-pitching. No open/click tracking (costs 8-12% inbox placement). Segments of <=50 beat blasts of 1000+ (~5.8% vs ~2.1%). Order of impact: deliverability > list relevance > copy.

## Campaign variety

Multiple prospects in one batch must differ in tone, structure and angle, not just swapped pain points. If the batch reads like one template with variables, rewrite it.

## Personalization

Good: their specific product/service/market, something visible on their site (not "I looked at your site"), their city's market, a current industry pressure. Bad: "you've clearly built something real", "solid reputation", "companies like yours", "as a {title}".
Fabricating any detail is disqualifying. If there is nothing specific, use their role's typical pain and keep it generic-honest rather than invent.

## Self-check before queueing ANY draft

1. Under the word cap for its slot? 2. Exactly one idea and one question? 3. Subject lowercase 2-5 words, honest? 4. Zero banned patterns and zero em dashes (grep for them)? 5. Starts with them, not I/We? 6. Every factual claim traces to persona.md or the prospect's own site/listing? 7. No product name in email 1? 8. Written in the prospect's language, naturally?

Any fail: rewrite, don't patch.
